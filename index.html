From paper forms to a system that just works2026 What I learned building a health records system for a city health office.

For our capstone, my team built a web-based records system for Dasmariñas City Health Office 2. Before it, everything ran on paper — patient records, appointments, and reports were all written by hand and kept in folders.

My job was the back-end and the database. The hardest part wasn't the code — it was understanding how the staff actually worked, then shaping the system around that instead of forcing them to change. We added role-based accounts so each person sees only what they need, and turned reports that used to take hours into a few clicks.

The biggest lesson: a system is only good if the people using it trust it. We spent real time on small things — clear labels, fast search, no surprises — because that's what makes someone choose the app over the old notebook.

Building Norte Cafe's online ordering flow2025 Menu, transactions, riders, and email alerts on a custom PHP MVC setup.

Norte Cafe is a web-based ordering and delivery system my team built for a café. A customer browses the menu, places an order, and that order moves through the kitchen and out to a rider — with the staff watching the whole thing from a dashboard.

We built it on a custom PHP MVC structure instead of a framework, mostly so we understood every moving part. The trickiest piece was keeping the order's state honest across everyone — customer, staff, and rider — so nobody sees stale information. Email alerts tied it together so no order gets missed.

If I did it again, I'd plan the database first and the screens second. Getting the data model right early would have saved us a lot of rework later.

Learning by shipping real projects2026 Why I'd rather build something small that works than wait until I feel ready.

I'm a BSIS student, but most of what I actually know came from building real systems — a café ordering app, an internship tracker, a health records system — not just from class.

Shipping teaches you things theory can't: how to talk to the person who'll use the app, how to cut scope when a deadline is close, how to fix a bug late at night without breaking three other things. Every project left me a little faster and a little calmer.

I'm still early, and that's fine. I'd rather build something small that genuinely helps someone than wait until I feel “ready.” Ready comes from doing.
